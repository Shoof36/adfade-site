AdFade Privacy Policy

Effective October 21, 2026

AdFade itself doesn't collect, sell or share personal data, and it has no account and no analytics. The free version shows ads from Google, described below; AdFade Pro has none.

Your TV

AdFade talks to the TVs on your own Wi-Fi with their makers' local control protocols (Roku, Samsung, LG and Vizio). What it needs to reconnect (each TV's address, name, pairing key, and the hardware address that lets Power turn it on) stays on your phone and goes nowhere else. On an iPhone it's part of your phone's own backup, like any app's settings.

Music

Radio streams play straight from the station's server to your phone. When you search for a station, the words you type go to radio-browser.info, a free public station directory, to find matching stations; nothing else about you goes with them. When you pick or play a station from the search, AdFade tells the directory which one (its station id), as the directory asks, so its list of popular stations stays current. Songs from your music library and your music app stay with Apple and that app: AdFade only asks them to play and pause.

The AdFade Break shortcut

When you add the shortcut, AdFade reads its current link from this website.

AdFade Pro

Subscriptions are sold and billed by Apple through the App Store, under Apple's privacy policy. AdFade never sees your payment details: it asks Apple, on your phone, whether your Apple ID has AdFade Pro.

Ads in the free version

On an iPhone without AdFade Pro, the Remote and Music tabs show one banner ad from Google AdMob. AdFade asks Google for ads that aren't personalized, and doesn't ask to track you across other companies' apps and websites. To show the ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google's ad code collects information such as your IP address (which can tell your general location), a device identifier, which ads were shown and tapped, and app performance and crash data, under Google's privacy policy. Where the law requires it (for example in the UK), Google asks for your consent first, and Settings → AdFade Pro → Ad Privacy Choices changes your answer. AdFade Pro removes the ads.

Your data and your choices

AdFade has no servers and keeps nothing about you anywhere but on your phone: its settings stay there, and deleting AdFade deletes them. Google keeps the data its ad code collects for as long as its privacy policy says, and gives you choices about it there. You can withdraw or change your ad consent in Settings → AdFade Pro → Ad Privacy Choices (where the law gives that choice), or remove the ads with AdFade Pro. The only others who get anything from AdFade are Google (for the ads) and radio-browser.info (for station searches); each gets only what this policy describes, and gives it the same or equal protection under its own privacy policy.
